Bengkalis Immigration: MILES Process Analysis

A business analysis case study of the mobile passport service run by Kantor Imigrasi Kelas II TPI Bengkalis. It maps the process, finds the pain points and proposes digitization improvements.

Built from one public document: the 2025 SOP package of the Bengkalis immigration office.

What this isA demonstration of process mapping, requirements writing and honest handling of evidence.
What this isn'tAn evaluation of how the office actually performs. It uses the published SOPs only, with no observation or staff interviews.

The process, before and after

MILES (Mobile Immigration Services) sends officers to schools, cafes, malls and companies to take photos and interview passport applicants. The text SOP describes 4 to 5 steps. The slide shows 17. Both diagrams below redraw that slide into swimlanes.

Key findings

  • MILES begins with a written request, and the SOP does not say whether it is paper.
  • The slide shows the team traveling before verification and the cekal (travel ban) check.
  • Nothing describes tracking after the passport goes to the post office.
  • The SOP document is inconsistent in several places.

Requirements

Only five, because the source is one SOP package and more would be invented. Payment stays after verification and the cekal check, which probably avoids refunds. Changes to national systems (M-Paspor, Simponi, SIMKIM) are out of scope.

IDThe solution shall...PriorityBasis
REQ-01Let an institution submit a MILES request online and receive a confirmation, instead of a written request.MustP1
REQ-02Review registrations before the MILES date and deploy only when a minimum number of applicants have complete documents.MustP2 · Assumption officers can see registrations early
REQ-03Show one status page from application to dispatch, including the PT Pos tracking number.ShouldP3 · Assumption PT Pos can supply tracking data
REQ-04Tell an applicant why documents were rejected or incomplete and how to reschedule.CouldP4 · Inferred
REQ-05Apply document control to the SOP package: version number, one named owner and a text-against-slide consistency check before publishing.ShouldP5

User stories

Each story traces to a requirement and a pain point, with acceptance criteria in Given/When/Then form.

US-01: Online MILES request (REQ-01, P1)

As an institution representative, I want to request a MILES visit online, so that I do not have to prepare and deliver a written request.

US-02: Review before the visit (REQ-02, P2)

As a Loket officer, I want to review applicants' registrations before the MILES date, so that the mobile team only travels when enough applicants are ready. The cekal check and final verification still happen on site.

US-03: One status page (REQ-03, P3)

As an applicant, I want one place to see my passport status through to delivery, so that I know when to expect it.

US-04: Clear rejection reasons (REQ-04, P4)

As an applicant, I want to know why my documents were rejected or incomplete, so that I can fix them without starting over.

US-05: Consistent SOPs (REQ-05, P5)

As the office head, I want every SOP checked for consistency before it is published, so that staff and the public are not given conflicting steps or times.

Limitations

  • The SOPs describe the intended process, not observed practice. Nothing was confirmed with staff.
  • Some text extracted from the PDF is garbled (notably pp.6-8), and the slides are low resolution.
  • Where the SOP contradicts itself, both versions are recorded.
  • There are no volumes, times beyond the stated standards, costs beyond the fee, or error rates.
  • Applicants with low digital skills could be excluded, so a manual or assisted channel must remain.
